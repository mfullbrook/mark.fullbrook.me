AI vendor data safety

Training, retention and UK residency across five vendors. Compiled from vendor documentation, September 2026. Verify before contracting.

Personal account, unsafe. Business account, safe. All five vendors exclude business data from training by default. On personal plans, four of the five train by default; Anthropic asks at signup.

Only Google processes in the UK today. Vertex AI in London, for allowlisted models. Microsoft has promised UK processing by the end of 2026. Anthropic and xAI offer no UK or EU residency self-serve.

On OpenAI, choose Europe — not United Kingdom. UK residency stores your data here but processes it elsewhere.

What the table cannot show

  1. Pick Europe, not the United Kingdom, on OpenAI. UK residency stores data here but processes it elsewhere. Processing residency exists for Europe, the US and the UAE only, and UK-to-EEA transfers are covered by UK adequacy regulations.
  2. The feedback button overrides everything. A thumbs up or down attaches the whole conversation and may send it for training, even where training is otherwise off. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI all document this.
  3. Coding agents have their own switches. OpenAI Codex has a separate full-environment training setting. Claude Code follows its account, so a developer on personal Pro is governed by their own private choice.
  4. Leaving the UK is lawful. UK GDPR does not require UK residency. Vendor DPAs carry the transfer safeguards; the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework is the alternative route. Check certification on the DPF register rather than assuming.
  5. Residency is never total. Authentication, routing, analytics, file text extraction, and anything reached through apps, MCP or web search sit outside the guarantee.
  6. Location is not jurisdiction. The US CLOUD Act lets American authorities compel a US company to hand over data it controls wherever it is stored. UK hosting by a US vendor changes where the disks are, not who can be ordered to open them.
  7. Courts override published policy. A New York Times court order forced OpenAI to preserve deleted chats; April–September 2025 data is still held. Enterprise and zero-retention API customers were excluded, as were UK and EEA conversations going forward.
  8. Microsoft quietly added Claude. In UK tenants created after 25 March 2026, Anthropic models are on by default in Word, Excel and PowerPoint Copilot, and that processing happens outside Microsoft's boundary. Older tenants should check the setting.
  9. Google's free developer tool is safer than its chat app. UK and EEA users of AI Studio get paid-tier data terms even on the free quota. The consumer Gemini app does not, and chats seen by human reviewers are kept for three years even after deletion. Gemini Notebook also ignores Workspace data-region settings.
  10. Microsoft offers no opt-out from human review. Consumer Copilot conversations can be read by reviewers when misuse is suspected, whatever your training setting.
  11. xAI will trade credits for your data. Its enterprise terms allow free credits in exchange for permission to train on business data. Grok used inside X has its own training setting, separate from the Grok app.

Sources: vendor privacy centres, terms and help documentation from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google (Workspace, Gemini Apps, Gemini API and Vertex AI), Microsoft (Copilot privacy FAQ and Microsoft 365 Copilot residency announcements) and xAI (consumer and enterprise FAQs, API regional endpoints); ICO guidance on the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

A starting point for a policy conversation, not legal advice. Vendor terms move quickly and several widely cited third-party summaries are already wrong.
